Savers have had to navigate a shifting rate environment over the last couple of years, but one thing hasn't changed: There are still opportunities to earn attractive returns on money that might otherwise sit in a low-rate deposit account. And while today's top savings rates aren't quite as high as they were at their recent peaks, some certificate of deposit (CD) accounts are still paying rates that are well above average.

That can make shorter-term CDs particularly appealing right now. With a 9-month CD, for example, you don't have to commit your money for multiple years to secure a competitive fixed interest rate. You'll know exactly what rate you're earning during the CD term, and your money will become available again in less than a year, giving you an opportunity to reassess your options relatively quickly.


How much interest can a $10,000 9-month CD earn right now?

What you earn on a 9-month CD depends largely on the annual percentage yield (APY) you're able to secure. Right now, some of the more competitive 9-month CDs have rates of 4.00% or higher, making it possible to generate a few hundred dollars in interest on a $10,000 deposit without taking on the risks associated with market-based investments.

What is magic lemon pie?
This classic lemon pie gets its “magic” from the way the filling sets up. The filling turns into a custard just by whisking the ingredients together, thanks to the acid from the freshly squeezed lemon juice reacting with the sweetened condensed milk. It might not be a no-bake recipe, but baking the crust and the entire pie results in an incredible texture and richer flavor. See—magic!

How to make magic lemon pie:
My version of magic lemon pie calls for an extra-buttery graham cracker crust that’s first partially baked before the filling is poured in. Why bake the crust? I found that pre-baking the crust gives the pie a jumpstart on caramelization—and caramelization is a very good thing. Once cooled and the filling is added, a second bake helps stabilize the pie along with ensuring the yolks are fully cooked.

The magic of molasses

While most common in sweet baked goods, molasses also shows up often in pulled-pork recipes. It’s a byproduct of the cane sugar-making process, which makes it less sweet than refined sugar. Indeed, molasses has a complex, slightly bitter, and smoky sweetness that complements barbecued meats. It lends nuance, helps develop a deeper color, and gives this recipe its deliciously sticky finish.
